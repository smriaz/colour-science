08 — Color difference (ΔE)

How different do two colors look? A common way to quantify that is ΔE (“delta E”), measured in a color space like Lab.

Big idea: RGB distance is not a good measure of “how different it looks.” Lab-based differences (ΔE) match perception much better.

ΔE is a “difference score”

  • Small ΔE → colors look almost the same
  • Bigger ΔE → easier to tell apart

We’ll try a game where you hunt for the “odd one out.”

ΔE76 vs ΔE2000

  • ΔE76: straight distance in Lab (simple and fast)
  • ΔE2000: improved model that better matches perception (especially in some regions)

In printing and quality control, ΔE thresholds are used to decide if a match is acceptable.

Why ΔE2000 exists

Lab isn’t perfectly uniform: equal distances don’t always look equally different. ΔE2000 adds weighting and corrections (hue/chroma/lightness interactions) to better approximate visual difference.

It’s widely used in color QA and printing specs. (We’ll implement it below.)

What you learned

Short recap.

  • ΔE is a way to quantify perceived color difference (usually using Lab).
  • ΔE76 is simple; ΔE2000 usually matches perception better.
  • Printing often uses ΔE tolerances for matching and quality control.
