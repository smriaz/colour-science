10 — CMYK & printing

Screens add light (RGB). Printing uses inks that subtract light from white paper. That’s why printers commonly use CMYK.

Big idea: Inks work by absorbing parts of the spectrum. Paper, ink chemistry, and lighting all matter — so CMYK→RGB “conversion” is only an approximation without a profile.

Why printing feels “opposite” of screens

  • Paper starts bright (white).
  • Ink makes it darker by blocking light.
  • More ink usually means less light comes back to your eyes.

Cyan, Magenta, Yellow are like “anti-RGB.”

Why CMYK has K (black)

  • CMY inks aren’t perfect; stacking them makes muddy darks.
  • Black ink improves shadows, detail, and reduces ink use.
  • In real workflows, conversion uses ICC profiles + rendering intents.

Subtractive mixing and device profiles

CMYK values are device-dependent: inks, paper, dot gain, screening, and press conditions vary. A meaningful CMYK↔Lab mapping requires characterization (profiles).

What you learned

Short recap.

  • Printing is subtractive mixing: inks absorb light from paper.
  • CMYK includes black (K) for better darks and efficiency.
  • CMYK is device-dependent — profiles are needed for accurate conversions.
