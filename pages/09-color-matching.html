09 — Color matching & metamerism

Many different spectra can look like the same color to us. That phenomenon is called metamerism.

Big idea: A match on one display or under one lamp may fail elsewhere because the underlying spectra are different — even if they look identical in one situation.

Same look, different “ingredients”

  • Two lights can look the same color.
  • But their rainbow “recipes” can be different.
  • Change the lamp or the viewer, and the match can break!

Why matching works (and why it breaks)

Your eye reduces a spectrum to three cone responses. If two spectra produce the same responses, they match (metamers). If conditions change (illumination, observer, device), the match can change.

Metamerism in printing

Inks have spectral reflectances. A proof and a press sheet may match under D50 but differ under LED lighting. Managing this involves measurement conditions, profiles, and sometimes choosing different inks/papers.

What you learned

Short recap.

  • Matching is possible because spectra collapse to ~3 cone responses.
  • Metamers match in one condition but can diverge in another.
  • This matters a lot for lighting, displays, and especially printing.
