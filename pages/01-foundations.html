01 — Foundations: What is color?

Color isn’t a “thing” inside objects. It’s something your brain builds from light, your visual system, and the context.

Big idea: what you see depends on the light shining on the object and how the object reflects or absorbs that light — plus how your eye/brain interprets it.

Color is a teamwork thing

  • Light comes in many kinds (like a rainbow).
  • Objects soak up some kinds of light and bounce others.
  • Your eyes + brain turn that bounced light into “color.”

Example: A red apple looks red in white light… but under blue light it can look dark or strange.

Color depends on illumination

We often say “this object is red,” but what we really mean is: under typical lighting it reflects more long-wavelength (reddish) light than other wavelengths.

  • Illuminant: the spectrum of the light source.
  • Reflectance: how the surface reflects each wavelength.
  • Perception: your cone responses + brain processing.

Color as an interaction: spectrum → cones → perception

A simplified pipeline is: spectral power distribution (light) × surface reflectance → retinal stimulus → cone excitations (L, M, S) → opponent processing → appearance.

This is why different spectra can sometimes look identical (metamerism) and why printing (ink spectra) can be tricky.
