02 — Light & spectra

Light isn’t just “bright” or “dim.” It has a recipe—how much energy it has at each wavelength. That recipe is called a spectrum.

Big idea: Two lights can look “white” but have very different spectra — and that can change how objects and prints look under them.

Think of light like a smoothie

  • A rainbow has many “flavors” of light.
  • Some lamps have lots of one flavor, not much of others.
  • Objects can only reflect what’s actually in the light!

Wavelength and “how much of each”

Visible light ranges roughly from ~380 nm (violet) to ~700 nm (red). A spectrum tells you the intensity across that range.

  • Sunlight is broad (lots of wavelengths).
  • Some LEDs can be spiky (strong peaks).
  • This affects color rendering (how “natural” colors look).

SPD: spectral power distribution

A light source is described by an SPD: power vs wavelength. Perceived color depends on how that SPD interacts with object reflectance and then projects onto cone sensitivities.

This sets up metamerism and printing issues: ink reflectances are spectral, so “same RGB under one lamp” may differ under another.

What you learned

Takeaways you’ll use again and again.

  • A spectrum is “how much light” at each wavelength.
  • Different light sources can look similar but have different spectra.
  • Spectra matter a lot for vision science (cones) and printing (ink reflectance).
